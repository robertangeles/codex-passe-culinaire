## Creative direction

A 75–90 second culinary film for chefs and restaurant owners. Follow one pear tart through a working day: the morning decision, ingredients drawn, skilled preparation, service and the evening reckoning. The restaurant feels worth caring for. CulinAIre Kitchen enters through the decisions and records that connect this work.

Photographic people, natural skin, practical kitchen movement and credible food. An intimate Melbourne restaurant with French culinary influence: morning window light, ivory tile, brushed steel, dark timber beyond the pass, folded linen and a restrained moss accent. Romance comes from light, food and care, not ornamental props or an implausibly spotless showroom. A functional commercial kitchen remains functional.

Use one fictional head chef, one fictional owner and a small supporting team, with consistent reference images, clothing and spatial layout. Hair is secured, sleeves controlled and work surfaces appropriate to each task. These people do not represent customers, the founder or testimonials.

## Shot construction

For each shot, use a consistent photographic reference, one principal physical action and a motivated camera position. Alternate medium views, working details and brief wide views. Avoid identical slow push-ins across every scene. Natural working speed is the default; no duration stretching, frozen holds to fill time, repeated loops or optical-flow repair of broken anatomy.

Keep faces and hands readable. Do not solve contact errors by cropping away all the skilled work. For difficult actions, use a clearer physical staging or another take. Retain a complete successful action before cutting. Model-generated writing and screens are replaced with controlled typography or illustrative interface inserts; use no fabricated product screenshot.

Deliver a muted-first film with optional sound only if produced and reviewed. The story must make sense without narration or sound. Use an original or properly licensed sound bed if audio is added. Do not invent speech endorsements.

## Quality gate before the full batch

Generate the three most revealing shots first: produce-crate lift, pastry preparation and oven loading. These test weight, grip, hands, food and physical contact. They are an internal production gate, not a replacement for the full film.

Watch every candidate at normal speed, then inspect critical contact frames. Reject drifting faces, changing clothing, fused fingers, object morphing, clipping, sliding feet, floating props, wrong knife technique and implausible oven contact. Confirm that movement feels purposeful rather than sluggish. Source resolution and frame rate must be recorded honestly; an upscale is not native capture detail.

Only proceed to the remaining shots when these candidates meet the standard. A more capable generator does not guarantee correct hands or kitchen technique. Record rejected takes and generation costs. At the approved spending limit, stop generation and report the evidence instead of silently purchasing more.
